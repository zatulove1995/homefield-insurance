The definition

What counts as an insurance lead?

An insurance lead from Homefield is a written reply from a homeowner in your territory expressing interest in your services — asking for a quote, asking what you would charge, or asking to be contacted. It is not a form fill, not a phone record, and not a name on a list. The homeowner read a short, plain-text email sent under your agency's identity and wrote back in their own words. The full reply thread arrives with the lead, so the first thing you read is exactly what they asked.

Homefield's data and timing are built around the house: the public-record closing date and the modeled home-policy renewal. We hold no auto, life, health, age, income or Medicare data and run no campaign built on them. Agencies that also write auto or life use the home renewal — and, for life, the new-mortgage moment — as the conversation starter in a sequence they approve. Personal-lines P&C, home first.

Timing

When the email lands: 30–45 days before the modeled renewal

Nobody outside the carrier knows a homeowner's renewal date, and we won't pretend otherwise. What we do know is when the home was purchased, because closing dates are public record, and home policies typically renew on the closing anniversary. Homefield models the renewal month from that and times the first email to land 30–45 days before it. Some sends drift early or late — a policy rewritten mid-term, a carrier switched last spring — and those homeowners still get a relevant note from a local agency; you are billed only when one writes back with interest. After major hail or wind in your territory, an additional sequence you approve can offer a coverage review, within state rules on post-storm solicitation.

Exclusivity

One agency per ZIP

"Exclusive" means two things here, and both are structural. Your ZIPs — 100 to 500 of them, chosen at onboarding, locked on signature — are closed to every other insurance agency in the Homefield network while your account is active and in good standing; a roofer, a real estate agent and an investor can hold the same ZIP, a second agency cannot. And the lead itself is a reply to your email, addressed to your agency, delivered to your agency, never resold — including a homeowner who circles back months later. How Homefield compares with shared-lead marketplaces

Shared-lead marketplaces sell the same consumer quote request to more than one agency — multiple buyers per lead, with each vendor's published cap, page and date listed on the cost guide. The vendors themselves say speed decides who wins: in a 2015 article for agents, EverQuote tells them to call within one minute of delivery, and SmartFinancial's agent guide says the first agent to make contact often has the strongest opportunity (their own pages, as of September 2026). An "exclusive" lead on a marketplace is a form fill that isn't resold (EverQuote for Agents FAQ, as of September 2026). A Homefield lead is exclusive in a different sense: the homeowner replied to your agency, the ZIP is locked to your agency, and no other buyer exists.

Where they still win

Instant volume on demand. If you need leads this afternoon, the marketplaces will sell you leads this afternoon — no setup period, no list build, budget up or down at will. We take about 21 days to the first lead. When speed matters more than exclusivity, they're genuinely the faster tap to open.

Billing

Pay per qualified lead — the model in one paragraph

You pay per qualified lead, plus a one-time onboarding fee — nothing else. The rate is set by industry and quoted to you in writing on the call, after your territory is checked live; it steps down when you pass 50 qualified leads in a calendar month and again when you pass 299. There is no platform fee, no prepaid deposit and no contract — cancel with 30 days' notice. Invoices arrive weekly, every Friday, in arrears, itemized with every reply thread, with a 7-day dispute window on any lead and a 30-day window on billing errors.

FAQ

Frequently asked questions about insurance leads

Which lines of insurance does Homefield run campaigns for?

The data and the timing model are built around the house — the public-record closing date and the modeled home-policy renewal — so home is the anchor line. We hold no auto, life, health, age, income or Medicare data and run no campaign built on them. Agencies that also write auto use the home renewal as the bundle moment (see the home + auto page); life-focused and multi-line agencies use the new-mortgage moment and the renewal, in a sequence they approve. Renters and commercial policies have no signal in a homeowner database, so there is no product for them.

Does this work for captive agents as well as independent agencies?

Both. The sequence runs under your office's identity from dedicated sending domains you approve at onboarding, and you approve every email before it sends. Captive agents should check their carrier's marketing guidelines before approving copy — the copy and its compliance are yours under our Terms. Fit is decided by territory (100–500 populated ZIPs) and same-day follow-up capacity, not by carrier model.

How is this different from a shared-lead marketplace?

A marketplace sells a consumer's online quote request to several agencies at once, in real time, and tells them to call fast. A Homefield lead is a homeowner who replied to an email sent in your agency's name, delivered to your agency only, in a ZIP locked to you, billed per qualified reply. They win on instant volume; we take about 21 days to the first lead. The published vendor facts and prices, with sources, are on what insurance leads cost.

Can I run Homefield alongside a marketplace?

Yes, and many agencies do: keep the marketplace on for volume, put Homefield next to it for 60 days, and compare cost per written policy in your own CRM. If you need leads this week, start with the marketplace — our first qualified leads arrive around day 21.
