The definition

What counts as a homeowners insurance lead here

A homeowners insurance lead from Homefield is a written reply, not a form fill. The homeowner received three short, plain-text emails signed by your agency, and answered one of them — asking for a quote, asking what you'd charge, or asking to be contacted. Every reply is read and graded before it reaches you; only the ones that show interest are delivered, and only those are billed.

A list is not a lead, and a form fill is not a request. A homeowner who typed a ZIP into a comparison site had not yet asked any agency a question. A homeowner who wrote "what would you quote us for the same coverage" to your agency, by name, has asked you a question — and the whole thread arrives with the lead so you can answer it in their words. The timing signal

How does Homefield know when a home policy renews?

Nobody outside the carrier knows the renewal date — not us, and not the X-date list vendors either. What is public is the day the home was purchased: closing dates sit in county recorder records, and home policies typically renew on the closing anniversary. Homefield models the renewal month from that date and schedules the first email to land 30–45 days before it, when the renewal notice is about to arrive and the household has usually not compared coverage since the year it bought the house.

Where the model drifts

Some of it drifts. A policy rewritten mid-term moves off its anniversary; a household that switched carriers last spring renews in spring now; a home bought with cash may have been insured on a different date. For those homeowners the email lands early or late — still a relevant note from a local agency, and still billed only if they reply with interest. We'd rather explain the model than claim data nobody sells.

How this differs from an X-date list

X-date vendors sell files. Dataman Group, for example, describes its homeowner X-date lists as using "the original home purchase date, to estimate insurance renewal timing," sold as records with minimum orders from $185 (datamangroup.com/homeowner-x-date-lists/, accessed 2026-09-14). That is the same public-record logic — and then the file is yours to mail or dial, sharing the ZIP with whoever else bought it. Homefield runs the outreach under your name, absorbs the non-responders, and hands you only the homeowners who wrote back. You receive replies, not a list.

The window is when households move. J.D. Power's 2024 U.S. Home Insurance Study measured a record 6.8% home-insurance shopping rate and found 21% of home customers would "definitely" switch home insurers if they switched auto (Insurance Business, 2024-09-18); its Q2 2025 shopping study put quarterly home switching at 2.5%, with bundlers shopping and switching at higher rates than non-bundlers (Insurance Business, 2025-07-23).

Territory

Exclusive home insurance leads mean one agency per ZIP

On a marketplace, "exclusive" means the form fill isn't resold. Here it means two things at once: the ZIP is locked to your agency against every other agency in the network while your account is active, and the lead is a reply addressed to you — there is no second buyer to race.

  • 100–500 ZIP codes per agency, locked on signature; under about 100 populated ZIPs is not a fit.
  • A roofer, an agent and an investor can share your ZIP — they're never quoting the same policy. A second agency cannot.
  • Cross-network de-duplication: a homeowner in your ZIPs is not contacted for a competing client.
  • Every reply in your ZIPs is yours, including the ones who circle back at next year's renewal.
  • Shared marketplaces, for contrast: the same quote request goes to multiple agencies — the vendor-published caps, with each vendor's page and date, are on the cost guide.

After a storm

After hail or wind in your ZIPs: a coverage-review wave

After a major wind or hail event in your territory we can run an additional sequence — separate from the renewal calendar — offering homeowners a coverage review while the roof and the policy are both on their minds.

  • You approve it like any other sequence, and it runs within state rules on post-storm solicitation. It offers a review, never a claim.
  • Included at no extra cost; storm-wave replies bill at your same per-lead rate.
  • Your ZIPs only. The roofer in a ZIP gets the estimate requests, the agency in the same ZIP gets the quote requests; neither is ever sold the other's reply.

Compliance

Compliance and where the data comes from

  • You are the sender; Homefield facilitates. You approve the sender identity, every sequence and the targeting; campaigns run at your direction, on your behalf, under your agency's identity. Ultimate responsibility for your marketing's compliance stays with your business, as our Terms spell out.
  • Every send identifies your agency, carries a physical postal address and a working unsubscribe honored promptly and suppressed permanently across every campaign, for every client.
  • Data: public records (county recorder/assessor) plus licensed data providers who certify lawful collection and permissible use, scrubbed against suppression lists before sending. We do not use sensitive attributes — age, credit, marital status or household earnings — to target anyone.
  • Phones are supplemental. We never represent Do Not Call status; you scrub before you dial. Where a homeowner asks to be contacted, the reply is your record of the request; whether and how you call is your decision under the TCPA and your state's rules.
  • Licensing. You hold your producer license; where a state requires the license number in marketing email, it goes in your approved sequence.
  • Email only. No calls, no texts, no consent forms, no live transfers.

FAQ

Frequently asked questions about home insurance leads

How do you know when a homeowner's policy renews?

We don't know the date — nobody outside the carrier does. We know the public-record closing date, and home policies typically renew on the closing anniversary, so we model the renewal month from it and send 30–45 days ahead. Some sends drift early or late when a policy was rewritten or a carrier changed; those homeowners still get a relevant note from a local agency, and you're billed only if they reply with interest.

What does a home insurance lead from Homefield actually say?

Whatever the homeowner wrote — most name it: "what would you quote us for the same coverage," "our premium jumped again, can you look," "send me what you need to compare." The full thread is attached to every lead, verbatim, so you're never guessing at what they meant.

Which filters build my list?

ZIP code (only the ones you locked), home value floor and ceiling, the modeled renewal window, home age, ownership length and owner-occupancy. Targeting is reviewed monthly and re-cut if quality dips.

What happens after hail or wind in my ZIPs?

We can run an additional sequence offering a coverage review — you approve it like any other sequence, it runs within state rules on post-storm solicitation, and replies bill at your normal per-lead rate. It offers a review, never a claim.

Can a roofer hold the same ZIP as my agency?

Yes. Territory is one business per industry per ZIP, so a roofer, a real estate agent and an investor can hold your ZIP — they're never quoting the same policy. A second insurance agency cannot. After a storm, the roofer gets the estimate requests and you get the quote requests; neither is sold the other's reply.

How is this different from an X-date list?

An X-date list is a file: records with an estimated renewal month that you then mail or dial, and that other buyers can license too. Homefield uses the same public-record logic but runs the outreach under your agency's name and delivers only the homeowners who wrote back, in ZIPs locked to you. You pay per qualified reply, not per record.

Can I use these leads to write home + auto bundles?

Yes, in follow-up. Your approved sequence can invite a home + auto bundle quote, and a homeowner asking what you'd charge is a bundle opportunity. Homefield holds no auto-policy or vehicle data and times nothing to an auto renewal — the timing signal is the home policy. The home + auto page spells out the boundary.

How fast do I need to respond to a home insurance lead?

Same day, ideally within the hour. In a Harvard Business Review audit of 2,241 U.S. companies, 23% never responded to a web lead at all and the average response time among those that did was 42 hours — and firms that responded within an hour were about seven times as likely to qualify the lead as those that waited even an hour longer (hbr.org, March 2011). Businesses using Homefield have reported 20–25% close rates with fast follow-up; results depend on your territory and your follow-up.
