Deep dive

How does Homefield compare with direct mail?

The economics of mail are simple and brutal: you pay full price for every homeowner who throws the postcard away, and more than 99% of them do. If you're an investor mailing absentee lists, you already know the math — and you know the same list is in four other mailboxes.

  • You pay for every non-responder. Print, postage, and list costs are spent before the first mailbox opens — the 99% who ignore you fund the campaign.
  • Response runs under 1%. A good drop is a fraction of a percent, and "response" often means a phone call you still have to convert.
  • You're dark for 2–4 weeks. No signal, no iteration, no idea whether the creative or the list is the problem until the money is gone.
  • There's no conversation. At best you get a phone number. A Homefield lead is a written reply you can respond to in their words.

Where mail still wins

Brand saturation. If your budget is effectively unlimited and the goal is every mailbox in the county seeing your name twelve times a year, mail does that and we don't. We put your name only in front of homeowners at the moment they're actually ready — narrower by design. If blanket awareness is the strategy, keep mailing.

Deep dive

How does Homefield compare with shared lead vendors?

EverQuote and QuoteWizard for insurance, Angi and Thumbtack for home services, Zillow for agents, resold absentee-owner lists and wholesalers for investors — the model is the same everywhere: one form fill or one name on a list, sold to several businesses at once, and the clock starts.

  • The multi-buyer race. The lead isn't yours — it's a head start you share with the other agencies that bought the same record: EverQuote's FAQ says up to three, QuoteWizard's up to four (their own pages, as of September 2026).
  • Speed-to-dial is the whole game. In a 2015 article for agents, EverQuote tells them to call within one minute; SmartFinancial says the first agent to make contact often has the strongest opportunity. You're not selling; you're winning a reflex contest.
  • Form-fill intent is thin. Someone clicked an ad and typed their ZIP to see a number. That's curiosity, not a request. A written reply asking what you'd charge is a request.
  • Prepaid deposits. Many marketplaces fund an account before the first lead arrives — MediaAlpha's minimum deposit is $250 and Datalot pre-pays by card, per their own pages as of September 2026 — so you're paying before you've seen a single lead's quality. We bill per qualified reply, weekly, in arrears.

Insurance agencies: the vendor facts and the 2026 price table, with sources, are on what insurance leads cost; the product itself is on insurance leads for agents.

Where vendors still win

Instant volume on demand. If you need leads this afternoon, they will sell you leads this afternoon — no setup period, no list build, dial the budget up or down at will. We take about 21 days to first lead. When speed matters more than exclusivity, they're genuinely the faster tap to open.

Deep dive

Against doing it yourself

Fair question, because every piece of this is buyable. Homeowner data, sending tools, reply handling — none of it is secret and none of it is exotic.

You could license homeowner records, stand up sending domains, and write three emails. Plenty of businesses have tried. The parts that look hard — the data, the tooling — aren't the parts that break.

What breaks is the operations layer underneath:

  • Sending reputation. Dedicated domains have to be set up, authenticated and kept in good standing every day — one misstep and your messages quietly stop arriving for weeks.
  • Reply classification at volume. Every reply has to be read and graded — interest, auto-reply, bounce, opt-out, hostile — fast enough that real interest gets to someone who can answer it while it's warm. At a few hundred replies a month this becomes someone's actual job.
  • The setup runway. New sending infrastructure takes weeks to establish before it's useful — one to two months of paying for tools and data with nothing to show for it. Our infrastructure is already established and in good standing, which is how first leads arrive around day 21 instead of month two.

When DIY is the right call

If you have an ops person who genuinely loves this work, consider it. The math can work — you'd keep our margin. What most businesses discover is that they don't have that person, and an owner doing sending-reputation triage between appointments is the most expensive version of "free." But if you do have them, we're not going to pretend the arithmetic says otherwise.

Fit

When we're not the right fit

We'd rather tell you on this page than bill you for three months first. If any of these describes your business, don't book the call.

Not a fit

Territories under ~100 ZIPs of real population

The model runs on volume. Below roughly 100 populated ZIP codes there simply aren't enough homeowners hitting their moment each month — a renewal, a roof at year 18, the equity to sell — to produce steady leads; you'd pay onboarding for a trickle. Rural businesses with wide, thin ground usually do better elsewhere.

Not a fit

No capacity to respond within a day

Leads decay. A homeowner who wrote back this morning expects an answer today — answer them same-day and you give yourself the best shot at the close rates businesses have reported; let the reply sit for three days and the moment is gone. If nobody on your team can turn replies around inside a day, the leads will be wasted on you.

Not a fit

Anyone who doesn't serve homeowners at their home

We do homeowners and their homes. That's the data, the timing model, and the copy — all of it is built around a person who owns a house and a decision about that house. If your customers are businesses, renters, or commercial property, our leads are worth nothing to you, and we won't sell them to you.

No switching required

Run us alongside what you already do

You don't have to believe our table. Keep your current channel running for 60 days, put us next to it, and track cost per acquired customer on both — your numbers, your CRM, not our marketing page. Then decide. There's no contract, so if we lose on your math, walking away costs nothing.
