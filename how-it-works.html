How it works

Three steps to start.
Then it compounds.

This page is the whole machine — the data, the timing signals, the sequence, the reply grading, and the clock from signature to your first qualified lead.

The short version

Three steps. The rest of this page is the detail.

  1. 01

    Claim your territory

    Pick 100–500 ZIP codes. Once they're yours, we won't sell them to another business in your industry — first come, first served, no exceptions.

  2. 02

    We run the outreach

    A personal three-email sequence, seven days apart, sent from dedicated sending domains registered for your campaign and approved by you at onboarding, signed by your business — timed to the moment a homeowner is actually ready: the policy about to renew, the roof hitting year 18, the equity that says it's time to sell.

  3. 03

    Replies land where you work

    An interested homeowner writes back, you get a Slack ping and a full record in your CRM within minutes. You take it from there. We keep sending.

A floor and a ceiling, so outreach matches the homes you actually want to work with.

How we know the moment

We don't — not exactly. Nobody outside the carrier knows the renewal date, nobody outside the household knows the roof is failing, and nobody knows a homeowner has decided to sell until they say so. Any vendor who tells you otherwise is selling you something.

What we do know is what public records tell us. Closing dates are public, and home policies typically renew on the closing anniversary — so we model the renewal month from that. Build year, permits, and property records tell us roughly how old a roof or an HVAC system is, so we model when it's aging into replacement range. Tenure, equity, absentee ownership, and life-stage signals tell us when a sale is realistic. Most of our outreach lands inside the window. Not all of it. A policy rewritten mid-term drifts off its anniversary, a roof gets replaced without a permit, an owner sells a year early — and for those homeowners the email arrives early or late instead of on time.

When it does, the email is still a relevant note from a local business to a homeowner who owns the house — and the ones who reply are still ready. We'd rather explain how the model works than pretend we have data nobody sells.

The sequence

Three short emails that read like a person wrote them.

Plain text, first person, your business's name on every one. No logos, no image banners, no "Dear Valued Homeowner." That's why people write back.

  • Three emails over 14 days — seven days apart, then the sequence ends
  • Sent from dedicated domains carrying your business's name — the homeowner is talking to you from the first sentence
  • Your own domain is never used for outreach — campaigns run on dedicated sending domains you approve at onboarding, so your everyday business email is unaffected
  • A reply exits the sequence instantly — nobody who answers ever gets another automated touch

Ellis Insurance Group

We're a local independent agency, and we'd be glad to run a comparison before the renewal lands. It takes about ten minutes and there's no obligation — just reply here and we'll get started.

— Jordan, Ellis Insurance Group

Insurance shown. Home services, agent, and investor sequences follow the same shape — same length, same plain-text register, a different moment.

Reply handling

Every reply gets read. Few get billed.

Replies come back to us first, where each one is read and graded before anything reaches you or your bill.

Disqualified automatically

  • Bounces and out-of-office auto-replies
  • Unsubscribes and opt-outs
  • Wrong contacts — moved, deceased, not the owner
  • Hostile replies and "no thanks"

None of these ever appear on an invoice. You pay for interest, not for volume.

Qualified leads, delivered in minutes

  • Your inbox — the full reply thread, verbatim, plus the enriched record
  • Slack — a ping the moment the reply grades qualified
  • Your CRM — pushed by webhook, no CSV exports, no copy-paste

A qualified lead is a homeowner asking for a quote, an estimate, a valuation, or an offer — asking what you'd charge, or asking to be called — in their own words.

Storm response

After severe weather in your territory, we run an additional outreach wave offering an inspection or coverage review — always within state rules on post-storm solicitation.

After a major wind or hail event in your territory, we run an additional outreach wave — separate from the regular calendar — while the roof and the coverage are both on homeowners' minds. It's the week people find the leak, discover their deductible, question their carrier, and actually answer email from a roofer or an insurance agency.

  • Included, no extra cost — storm-wave replies are billed at your same per-lead rate
  • Roofing and insurance — the roofer in a ZIP gets the estimate requests, the agency in the same ZIP gets the quote requests; neither is ever sold the other's reply
  • Your ZIPs only — the wave runs inside your locked territory, so every reply it produces is yours

Compliance

Compliance, handled.

Commercial email to homeowners is legal under CAN-SPAM and needs no prior consent — unlike cold calls and texts, which fall under the TCPA. Here's what we do to keep every send clean.

Real identity

Every email identifies the actual sender — your business, by name. No misleading subject lines, no spoofed headers.

Physical address

A physical mailing address appears in every message, exactly as CAN-SPAM requires.

One-click unsubscribe

Honored instantly. One click and the contact never hears from the campaign again.

Suppression & state rules

Opt-outs go onto a permanently maintained suppression list, and state-specific rules are respected wherever they're stricter.

The consent gap matters: TCPA rules make cold calls and texts to homeowners legally risky, which is one reason we send email and only email.
