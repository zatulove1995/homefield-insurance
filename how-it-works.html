How we know when a policy renews

We don't — not exactly. Nobody outside the carrier does, and any vendor who tells you otherwise is selling you something.

What we do know is when the home was purchased, because closing dates are public record — and home policies typically renew on the closing anniversary. We model the renewal month from that. Most of our outreach lands inside the 30–45 day window. Not all of it. A policy that was rewritten mid-term drifts off its anniversary, and for those homeowners the email arrives early or late instead of on time.

When it does, the email is still a relevant note from a local agency to a homeowner who pays for insurance — and the ones who reply are still shopping. We'd rather explain how the model works than pretend we have carrier data nobody sells.

The sequence

Three short emails that read like a person wrote them.

Plain text, first person, your agency's name on every one. No logos, no image banners, no "Dear Valued Homeowner." That's why people write back.

  • Three emails over 14 days — seven days apart, then the sequence ends
  • Sent from warmed domains carrying your agency's name — the homeowner is talking to you from the first sentence
  • Your real domain is never touched — zero deliverability risk to the email you run your business on
  • A reply exits the sequence instantly — nobody who answers ever gets another automated touch

Reply handling

Every reply gets read. Few get billed.

Replies come back to us first, where each one is read and graded before anything reaches you or your bill.

Disqualified automatically

  • Bounces and out-of-office auto-replies
  • Unsubscribes and opt-outs
  • Wrong contacts — moved, deceased, not the owner
  • Hostile replies and "no thanks"

None of these ever appear on an invoice. You pay for interest, not for volume.

Qualified leads, delivered in minutes

  • Your inbox — the full reply thread, verbatim, plus the enriched record
  • Slack — a ping the moment the reply grades qualified
  • Your CRM — pushed by webhook, no CSV exports, no copy-paste

A qualified lead is a homeowner asking for a quote, asking what you'd charge, or asking to be called — in their own words.

Storm response

When hail hits your ZIPs, we're already writing.

After a major wind or hail event in your territory, we run an additional outreach wave — separate from the renewal calendar — while coverage is on homeowners' minds. It's the week people discover their deductible, question their carrier, and actually answer email about insurance.

  • Included, no extra cost — storm-wave replies are billed at your same per-lead rate
  • Your ZIPs only — the wave runs inside your locked territory, so every reply it produces is yours

Compliance

Compliance, handled.

Commercial email to homeowners is legal under CAN-SPAM and needs no prior consent — unlike cold calls and texts, which fall under the TCPA. Here's what we do to keep every send clean.

Real identity

Every email identifies the actual sender — your agency, by name. No misleading subject lines, no spoofed headers.

Physical address

A physical mailing address appears in every message, exactly as CAN-SPAM requires.

One-click unsubscribe

Honored instantly. One click and the contact never hears from the campaign again.

Suppression & state rules

Opt-outs go onto a permanently maintained suppression list, and state-specific rules are respected wherever they're stricter.

The consent gap matters: TCPA rules make cold calls and texts to homeowners legally risky, which is one reason we send email and only email.
